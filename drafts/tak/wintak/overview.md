## The OpenDefence Standard Model

There are many ways to use TAK; we offer a thought-through usage model that delivers value without complexity. TAK collects information from the force and distributes it back as shared situational awareness. Two roles carry it: the **fighter** and the **command post (HQ)**.

## Using TAK as a fighter

- Keep your own position live on the map so others see you (blue force tracking).
- When you make an observation, create a marker and **send it to the HQ role**.
- Follow the **RECON Feed** — your command post creates and maintains it for your unit.
- Report and communicate over the prescribed messaging tools on their channels.

## Being a command post user

- Create and maintain the **RECON Feed** for the whole unit: receive observations, process them, and send the processed markers back to the feed.
- Build situational awareness — understand what is happening now, in 6 hours, and in 24 hours.

## Command Post — continuous tasks

The command post continuously, among other things:

- **Knows** the force's mission and execution phase to squad and team precision.
- **Receives** immediately-reportable matters and periodic situation reports from the force.
- **Maintains** a message journal and a numbered intelligence diary.
- **Maintains** the information systems, of which TAK is one.

TAK is just one tool — use it to collect and share information, not to capture your attention.
